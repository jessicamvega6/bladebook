Erasure by Constant

Every identifier on a surviving record is overwritten with the same literal "DELETED". The money and the dates stay. Because every row carries the identical value, no row can be linked to any other — and that unlinkability, not the removal of the name, is what makes this erasure.

Read the middle band. Nothing crosses it, and nothing runs between the three cards below it. Those rows are three separate islands — the coach's revenue still totals correctly, and no query, join or eyeball can establish that any two of them concern the same child.

Two keys that need handling, not just attributes

Overwriting attributes is not sufficient on its own, because the seat's own key and two index keys embed the student identifier directly.

SK = SEAT#<date>#<time>#<studentId> — must be re-keyed

Added 2026-10-13 for ADR-031 (lessons and seats). A seat's sort key contains the student identifier, so anonymising means deleting the seat and writing it again under a new key, in one transaction. The replacement can't be a single shared constant: two erased skaters who shared a lesson would collide on the same key. Each gets a numbered suffix (DELETED-1, DELETED-2). SEATEND# records are handled the same way.

GSI1PK = CLIENT#<clientId> — must be removed

Seats are indexed by family for the client-facing views (ADR-022, ADR-031). Blanking the attribute while leaving the key in place would keep every lesson retrievable under the family's identifier. Deleting the two GSI1 attributes drops the row out of the index entirely, which is exactly the intent.

GSI3SK = UNPAID#<date>#<studentId> — resolves itself

The unpaid index also embeds the identifier, but it is sparse and written only while a seat is UNPAID. A live debt cannot be anonymised anyway — you would lose the ability to collect it — so by the time erasure is permitted the seat is PAID or WAIVED and has already dropped out of GSI3. The awkward case is closed by an ordering rule rather than by code.

One identifier is easy to miss

CHANGE_BATCH.cancelledById holds the acting identity. When a client cancels their own lesson, that is their clientId — so the batch header carries identity even though the batch itself is about a schedule change, not a person. It needs the same treatment as the lines beneath it.

What this costs

The coach loses per-student history. "Mia paid me $2,400 last year" is no longer answerable, because answering it requires exactly the linkage this option destroys. Annual and per-date totals are unaffected — and totals are what a tax record needs.

That trade is the honest version of the promise in §7. It should be stated to the coach in those words before they confirm, not discovered afterwards.
